Overview & virology

Hantaviruses are enveloped, segmented, negative-sense RNA viruses in the order Bunyavirales, family Hantaviridae. Clinically important viruses are mainly orthohantaviruses carried by rodents. The genome has S, M, and L segments encoding nucleocapsid, glycoproteins, and RNA polymerase.

Human disease follows inhalation or mucosal contact with infectious rodent excreta. Viral entry involves cellular attachment factors, endocytosis, membrane fusion, cytoplasmic replication, and immune-mediated capillary leak affecting lungs or kidneys.

Zoonotic pattern

Each major strain is linked to a reservoir species: deer mice for Sin Nombre virus, bank voles for Puumala virus, brown/black rats for Seoul virus, and long-tailed pygmy rice rats for Andes virus. Rodents usually have chronic, mostly asymptomatic infection and shed virus in urine, feces, and saliva.

Exception: Andes virus can rarely spread person-to-person during close contact while a person is symptomatic.

Discovery timeline

A Korean War illness was later connected to the Hantaan River region. Hantaan virus was isolated in the 1970s, and the 1993 Four Corners outbreak led to recognition of Sin Nombre virus and hantavirus pulmonary syndrome in North America.

Old World viruses mainly cause HFRS; New World viruses mainly cause HPS/HCPS, though overlap and exceptions exist.

Prevention toolkit

Homeowners

  • Seal holes larger than 1/4 inch.
  • Store food and trash in rodent-proof containers.
  • Ventilate closed rooms for 30 minutes before cleanup.
  • Do not sweep dry droppings; wet with disinfectant/bleach solution, then wipe.

Campers & travelers

  • Avoid cabins with rodent signs until cleaned safely.
  • Keep tents zipped; avoid sleeping directly on ground.
  • Use sealed food storage.
  • In South America, avoid close contact with anyone ill after Andes-virus exposure.

Researchers & workers

  • Use employer-approved PPE and respiratory protection.
  • Follow biosafety plans for rodent handling.
  • Report febrile illness after exposure.
  • Use trained cleanup/disinfection procedures.

Vaccine tracker, 2026

  • Licensed/regional: inactivated HFRS vaccines used in parts of Asia; coverage and durability vary.
  • mRNA candidates: preclinical/early development for Hantaan and broader antigens.
  • Andes/HPS vaccines: no broadly approved civilian vaccine as of this build.
  • Open questions: correlates of protection, pan-hantavirus neutralization, rodent ecology forecasting, early host-response biomarkers.
